# Dishari Fashion Show 2026 — Master Script

## Beyond the Runway: Roots That Travel

**Document status:** Living working draft  
**Current version:** 0.18 — August 26, 2026  
**Target duration:** 32–35 minutes  
**Cast:** 20–21 adults, mostly Indian adults ages 35–55; approximately 4–5 men  
**Rehearsals:** 5–6  

> This is the single source of truth for the production. New decisions and revisions should be incorporated here. Material removed during development should be retained briefly in the Revision Notes until the replacement is approved.

**Animation companion:** `Dishari_Formation_Rehearsal_Hub.html`. Open the hub separately and choose the matching act/scene. Markdown cannot reliably run interactive HTML inside the document; the final Google Doc should use inline looping GIFs with a link to the hub.

## Creative rules

- This remains a fashion show: runway walks and clothing must dominate.
- Story is communicated through formation, projection, music, one clear gesture, and short voiceover—not extended acting.
- Every model receives an individual or paired hero walk.
- Avoid difficult dance and long synchronized sequences.
- Control visual density: use solo or paired light for declarations, let completed walkers exit or wait in blackout, and reserve all-seven formations for story reveals and scene endings.
- Reuse a small formation vocabulary: two diagonals, corridor, V, staggered line, full-cast tableau.
- *Dhurandhar* influences the dossier language, tension, swagger, entrances, and identity-reveal styling; the soundtrack will use multiple musical worlds.
- Act 1 draws from the emotional and classical-crossover worlds of *Swades* and *Bandish Bandits*.
- Act 2 uses urban rebel hip-hop, potentially Hanumankind-style music.
- Act 3 provides the grand cinematic fusion climax.
- Later, each model will receive an AI rehearsal video showing entry cue, route, formation mark, prop/reveal, pose, and exit.

# Act 1 — Arrival & Survival

## Chapter One: The Weight We Carried

### New Land. Old Self.

**Status:** Revised working concept  
**Structure status:** LOCKED — scene order, featured-model assignments and timestamps will not change; poses, emotions and prop details may still be refined.  
**Duration:** 8–8½ minutes  
**Show time:** 1:30–10:00  
**Participants:** Mustufa, Jaya, Preeti, **TBD**, Akash, Prerna, Yogita  
**Props:** Two medium vintage-style suitcases; two long Indian textile panels packed inside; optional winter accessories already worn by models  
**Costumes:** Indian or Indo-Western base looks covered partially by practical coats, cardigans, neutral shawls or travel layers

## Story

Seven immigrants make the same journey at different times. Two suitcases represent the luggage shared by an entire generation—not two specific travelers. The immigrants first appear in practical travel layers. They cross into the unfamiliar new country in pairs, face recognizable survival chapters, and finally open the two suitcases to reveal what could not be left behind: color, memory and identity.

The suitcases are scenic anchors. They are rolled on once, remain at two fixed upstage corners, and are opened only for the main transformation. Models do not repeatedly carry, exchange or search through them.

## Stage layout

- This production uses the entire width and depth of a large high-school auditorium stage; there is no narrow runway or audience aisle.
- Divide the stage into nine rehearsal zones: upstage-left, upstage-center, upstage-right; center-left, center, center-right; downstage-left, downstage-center, downstage-right.
- Suitcase 1 remains at the upstage-left corner and Suitcase 2 at the upstage-right corner. Both should be elevated slightly or lit clearly enough to remain visible from the auditorium.
- The center lane and both broad diagonals remain clear for traveling walks.
- Models should use long full-stage paths, allowing costumes to be seen from multiple angles. Poses are held at downstage-left, downstage-center and downstage-right rather than at one runway endpoint.
- Large screen titles identify every chapter so the story does not rely on facial acting.
- Final positions use two clean corner-to-center diagonal lines, not a staggered cluster.

## Music direction

- Opening: cinematic travel ambience or a restrained *Yeh Jo Des Hai Tera*-inspired instrumental section.
- Departure walks: the forward movement of *Yun Hi Chala Chal* or a contemporary journey remix.
- First Winter: cinematic wind, restrained orchestral rhythm and a clear walking pulse.
- First Home: Indian street soundscape, a hard cut to silence, then a minimal clock/breathing pulse.
- First Festival: classical vocals and percussion building from one small sound into communal celebration.
- Suitcase reveal and finale: *Garaj Garaj*, the *Bandish Bandits* theme, or another classical-crossover percussion climax.
- This act should feel emotionally Indian and cinematic; it does not need *Dhurandhar* graphics or terminology.

## Practical staging rules

- Do not put loose small objects inside the suitcases.
- Do not close the suitcases after the reveal.
- Tape the two suitcase positions and all crossing start/stop marks on the floor.
- Tape and name all nine stage zones so rehearsal instructions use consistent language.
- Start the second crossing model four counts later to prevent a center collision.
- Use the entire stage width and depth; avoid repeatedly funneling everyone through downstage-center.
- Use diagonal crossings for travel and broad lateral or curved routes during the survival chapters.
- The only shared handling is Mustufa/Jaya opening the suitcases and extending the textiles.
- If long textiles prove difficult, replace them with two pre-folded 6–8 foot dupattas or banners.

# Act 2 — Fitting In & Standing Out

## Identity: Declassified

### Blend In. Until You Break Out.

**Show time:** 10:30–19:15  
**Duration:** 8:45  
**Structure status:** LOCKED — scene order, featured-model assignments and timestamps will not change; poses, emotions and prop details may still be refined.  
**Participants:** Kanika, Advaito, Neha, Pooja, Brinto, Pooja (Jag), **TBD**  
**Formation captains:** Kanika, Pooja and **TBD** — assign the strongest walkers  
**Props:** reversible name badges and two empty photo frames  
**Costumes:** coordinated Western neutrals concealing bold fusion details

## Story

Seven immigrants learn to look polished, agreeable and easy to pronounce. They move through corporate lines and picture-perfect frames until one person stops editing their identity. The others follow. Their controlled walks become bolder, their full names return, and the act ends with individuality—not aggression—taking command of the stage.

Scenes 1–4 belong to one featured pair or solo. The full group returns only in Scene 5, where the rotating diamond and final V provide the act's signature formation sequence.

### Scene 1 — Fitting-In Protocol

**Animation cue:** Rehearsal Hub → Act 2 → Scene 1

| 0:20–0:50 | Short voiceover | They pass four counts apart, stop at DL/DR and perform matching gestures: straighten badge, adjust cuff, then face front. |

**Voiceover**

**Performance rule:** Movement is crisp, angular and deliberately programmed: straight backs, squared shoulders, fixed forward gaze, sharp beat-led turns and identical approved poses. Faces remain controlled rather than theatrical. No soft hand gestures, mutual glances or expressive interaction. The visual story is that individuality is being suppressed to appear acceptable.

# Act 3 — Desi Heart, Global Mind

## Operation Belonging

### The Mission Was Never to Choose.

**Show time:** 19:45–28:30  
**Duration:** 8:45

**Participants:** Ria, Monali, Sunny, Joyeta, Moitri, Bhanu, Tirna

## Story

The final seven refuse the demand to choose between being Indian and being American. Their clothes and walks present identity as a confident combination rather than a compromise. Separate paths gradually join into one ceremonial court: different expressions, shared roots, and complete belonging.
